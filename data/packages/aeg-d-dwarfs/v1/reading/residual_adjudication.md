# Residual conversion inputs: comparison

Two independent readings under `RESIDUAL_READING_BRIEF.md` (`residual_reading_1.csv`, 106 rows; `residual_reading_2.csv`, 85 rows).

**Agreement on every number used.** The readers agree on every value and error listed below.
- **Muñoz 2018, Table 1 adopted heliocentric distance:** all 20 systems. The table prints no errors. The only difference between the readers is that reader 2 labelled the values `headline` and reader 1 labelled them `adopted`.
- **McConnachie 2005 moduli:** And VI 24.47 ± 0.07, And VII 24.41 ± 0.10. McConnachie 2006 prints M_V of −11.5 ± 0.2 and −13.3 ± 0.3, "corrected" using the McConnachie 2005 distances (quote in both readings).
- **Medina 2018:** Leo IV 151.4 ± 4.4 kpc and Leo V 169.0 ± 4.4 kpc, RR Lyrae means. The earlier reading's "not printed" was wrong, because the values are printed in kpc. No modulus is printed.
- **Stetson 2014 (Leo I):** a range, 22.06 ± 0.08 to 22.25 ± 0.07 depending on the assumed metallicity. No single adopted value.
- **Simon 2020 (Tucana V):** M_V,0 = −1.1 (+0.5/−0.6) and distance 55 (+3/−8) kpc, both in Table 6. No sentence ties the two together.
- **van de Rydt 1991 (Phoenix):** the adopted modulus is (m−M)₀ = 23.1 ± 0.1 and M_V = −9.9 ± 0.4. It is unclear whether M_V uses that modulus, since it comes from matching luminosity functions to Fornax and M3.
- **de Jong 2008 (Leo T):** M_V ≃ −8.0, with no error printed. The modulus behind it is not stated. The candidates are the headline 23.1 ± 0.2 from the red clump, 23.0 ± 0.2 from the MATCH fit, and 23.1 assumed from the discovery paper.

**Not stated in any of these papers:** which modulus the printed M_V uses, except McConnachie 2006. These cases go to JvH as D-061.
