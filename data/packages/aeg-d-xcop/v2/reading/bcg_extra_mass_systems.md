# X-COP v2 second reading: which central galaxies E22 l. 109's "8.3 ± 2.9 per cent" describes (part B item 7, `bcg_ml_extra_mass_model`)

**Outcome: decided — A644 and A2319 only.** `applies_to` = `aeg-s-000177;aeg-s-000183` (the test's own IDs for A644 and A2319, item 7). This differs from the test's default (the five).

## 1. The E22 sentences (arXiv 2205.01110v1, `hydromass.tex`, SHA-256 `fdf040686a41eee38be2b9a2feb28c1c97b0b8cc0a4c8a7aa78785947cfa5cb1`)

- **l. 107** (all five BCGs): "In addition to the stellar mass component, the BCG mass models include a central mass component for a supermassive black hole, and a DM halo that follows a spherically symmetric NFW profile. The DM mass ($M_{\rm DM}$) within $R_{200}$ was approximated from weak lensing results \citep{herbonnet2020}, resulting in only two free parameters"
- **l. 109** (the whole line): "For A644 and A2319, we did not have weak lensing masses available from \citet{herbonnet2020} but used the average DM distribution (of the 23 other clusters in \citealt{loubser20}) to calculate the effect of a DM mass component on the best fitting stellar mass-to-light ratio ($\Upsilon_{* \rm DYN}$). When a DM mass component is included in the dynamical mass models for the BCGs, the best fitting $\Upsilon_{* \rm DYN}$ decrease by 8.3 $\pm$ 2.9 per cent on average."

Read alone, l. 109's second sentence could describe all BCGs. The paragraph's subject is A644 and A2319, and l. 107 already gives the other three their own weak-lensing DM component. E22 does not say over which clusters the average is taken, so its source decides.

## 2. The source of the figure (Loubser et al. 2020, arXiv 2006.05706v1, `BCG_v3_withoutBold.tex`, SHA-256 `861938c060fe6e1dfad851652781f44a6fad55b1a812fc9897772950f3b89e26`; e-print `ded65c6d1457b3183ee21b573bf426bf5ddfd20575fc39f429eb0e887dd9044c`, equal to readings §3)

- **l. 458:** "We do the dynamical modelling of the remaining 25 BCGs, but for Abell 644 and 2319 we do not model the case with dark matter included ($\star$ + CEN + DM) since no weak lensing masses are available in \citet{Herbonnet2019}."
- **l. 475:** "Adding the $M_{\rm DM}$ mass component decreases $\Upsilon_{\star \rm DYN}$ on average by 8.3 $\pm$ 2.9 per cent over our kinematic range, and increases $\beta_{z}$ by on average 0.04 (Abell 2055 and Abell 963 not included)."
- **l. 684** (conclusions) repeats: "Adding the halo mass component decreases $\Upsilon_{\star \rm DYN}$ on average by 8.3 $\pm$ 2.9 per cent over our kinematic range".
- **Table `DynModsTable`** (l. 503–537, caption l. 505: "For Abell 644 and Abell 2319, no weak lensing mass are available."): the ★+CEN+DM fits list Abell 1795 (l. 522), Abell 2029 (l. 525) and Abell 2142 (l. 528), and neither A644 nor A2319.

## 3. Reading

- The 8.3 ± 2.9 % is Loubser's average over the BCGs modelled **with** a weak-lensing DM halo (25 modelled, minus A644 and A2319, minus A2055 and A963 per l. 475). A644 and A2319 are not in that average.
- A1795, A2029 and A2142 have their own DM-included fits (E22 l. 107; Loubser table). For them the effect of the DM component is in their individual Υ, not an applied average.
- For A644 and A2319, E22 l. 109 says the effect of a DM component was calculated from the other clusters' average, and the sentence that follows gives that average. **So the figure is the size of an averaged adjustment that applies to A644 and A2319 only.**

Stated once: E22 does not literally say "we lowered Υ by 8.3 % for A644 and A2319". The reading rests on the paragraph's subject plus Loubser l. 458 and l. 475. It is not "not decidable", because the two sources together leave one consistent reading.

Minor: E22 says "the 23 other clusters"; Loubser's average excludes two more (A2055, A963), so it is over 21. Recorded, no effect.
