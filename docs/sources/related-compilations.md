## A. Cross-scale compilations (several kinds of system on one plot)

### A1. Lelli, McGaugh, Schombert & Pawlowski 2017, "One Law To Rule Them All: The Radial Acceleration Relation of Galaxies"
- Journal: ApJ (per comments: "Accepted for publication in ApJ"); DOI 10.3847/1538-4357/836/2/152
- arXiv: 1610.08981 https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.08981
- Quote: "LTGs, ETGs, and "classical" dSphs follow the same radial acceleration relation"
- What it compiles: 240 galaxies, namely 153 SPARC late-type galaxies, 25 early-type galaxies (ATLAS3D stellar+HI, or Chandra X-ray) and 62 dwarf spheroidals.
- Framing: reports the empirical relation. The abstract describes it as "tantamount to a Natural Law" and poses the question as "the link between baryons and dark matter".
- Data released: not stated.
- Fetched: 2026-11-06

### A2. Tian, Ko, Li, McGaugh et al. 2024, "A Distinct Radial Acceleration Relation across Brightest Cluster Galaxies and Galaxy Clusters"
- Journal: A&A 684, A180 (2024)
- arXiv: 2402.12016 https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12016
- Quote: "we systematically explored the acceleration and mass correlations between dynamical and baryonic components in 50 Brightest Cluster Galaxies (BCGs)"
- What it compiles: the dynamical RAR of 50 BCGs from MaNGA integral-field kinematics, set against the lensing RAR on BCG–cluster scales. This combines two channels across two scales.
- Framing: reports the empirical relation; it finds the BCGs consistent with a cluster-scale relation that has a larger acceleration scale.
- Data released: not stated.
- Fetched: 2026-11-06

### A3. McGaugh, Mistele, Duey, Haubner et al. 2026, "The Baryonic Mass-Halo Mass Relation of Extragalactic Systems"
- Journal: ApJ, accepted (per comments)
- arXiv: 2603.06479 https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.06479
- Quote: "Our sample covers nine orders of magnitude in baryonic mass, including galaxies with kinematic or weak gravitational lensing data and groups and clusters"
- What it compiles: baryonic mass against enclosed dynamical mass M200, from galaxies to rich clusters, using dynamics and lensing. This is an equivalent global (mass-ratio) plot, not a radial g_obs–g_bar plot.
- Framing: reports an empirical relation and fits a functional form (f_b tanh(M_b/M_0)^(1/4)). It compares the result with abundance-matching relations.
- Data released: not stated.
- Fetched: 2026-11-06

## B. Single-scale compilations that extended the relation to a new regime

### B1. McGaugh, Lelli & Schombert 2016, "The Radial Acceleration Relation in Rotationally Supported Galaxies"
- Journal: Phys. Rev. Lett. 117, 201101 (2016)
- arXiv: 1609.05917 https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.05917
- Quote: "The same relation is followed by 2693 points in 153 galaxies with very different morphologies, masses, sizes, and gas fractions."
- Regime: disc galaxies (rotation curves, SPARC). This is the reference definition of the RAR.
- Framing: reports the empirical relation. The abstract states that "the dark matter contribution is fully specified by that of the baryons".
- Data released: not stated.
- Fetched: 2026-11-06

### B2. Brouwer, Oman, Valentijn, Bilicki et al. 2021, "The Weak Lensing Radial Acceleration Relation: Constraining Modified Gravity and Cold Dark Matter theories with KiDS-1000"
- Journal: A&A 650, A113 (2021)
- arXiv: 2106.11677 https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.11677
- Quote: "These measurements extend the radial acceleration relation (RAR) by 2 decades into the low-acceleration regime beyond the outskirts of the observable galaxy."
- Regime: weak lensing around isolated galaxies (KiDS-1000).
- Framing: measures the lensing RAR, then tests named accounts: MOND, Verlinde's emergent gravity, and the ΛCDM simulations MICE and BAHAMAS. It reports a difference of at least 6σ between early- and late-type galaxies.
- Data released: not stated.
- Fetched: 2026-11-06

### B3. Mistele, McGaugh, Lelli, Schombert et al. 2024, "Radial acceleration relation of galaxies with joint kinematic and weak-lensing data"
- Journal: JCAP 04 (2024) 020
- arXiv: 2310.15248 https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.15248
- Quote: "We find that the RAR inferred from weak-lensing data smoothly continues that inferred from kinematic data by about 2.5 dex in acceleration."
- Regime: weak lensing to large radii, joined to kinematics. It uses a new deprojection method.
- Framing: reports the empirical relation and introduces a new method. It finds that early- and late-type galaxies lie on one joint RAR under strict isolation, which contrasts with B2.
- Data released: not stated.
- Fetched: 2026-11-06

### B4. Tian, Umetsu, Ko, Donahue et al. 2020, "The Radial Acceleration Relation in CLASH Galaxy Clusters"
- Journal: ApJ 896, 70 (2020)
- arXiv: 2001.08340 https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.08340
- Quote: "we examine if such a correlation exists in galaxy clusters using weak-lensing, strong-lensing, and X-ray data sets available for 20 high-mass clusters"
- Regime: galaxy clusters (lensing + X-ray).
- Framing: fits a power law (slope 0.51, scale g‡ = 2.02e-9 m s^-2) and concludes there is "no universal RAR that holds on all scales". It compares the result with a ΛCDM semi-analytical model.
- Data released: not stated.
- Fetched: 2026-11-06

### B5. Eckert, Ettori, Pointecouteau, van der Burg et al. 2022, "The gravitational field of X-COP galaxy clusters"
- Journal: A&A 662, A123 (2022)
- arXiv: 2205.01110 https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.01110
- Quote: "the relation between observed and baryonic acceleration exhibits a complex shape that strongly departs from the radial acceleration relation in spiral galaxies"
- Regime: galaxy clusters (X-ray + SZ hydrostatic, 12 clusters). The baryons are split into gas, BCG and satellite galaxies.
- Framing: fits a model (NFW) and compares with ΛCDM predictions. It reports the g_obs–g_bar relation at cluster scale.
- Data released: not stated in this abstract. The companion data paper is C4.
- Fetched: 2026-11-06

### B6. Li, Tian, Júlio, Pawlowski et al. 2023, "Measuring galaxy cluster mass profiles into the low acceleration regime with galaxy kinematics"
- Journal: A&A 677, A24 (2023)
- arXiv: 2303.10175 https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.10175
- Quote: "our approach probes accelerations as low as $10^{-11}$ m s$^{-2}$, comparable to the outskirts of individual late-type galaxies."
- Regime: galaxy clusters, from member-galaxy dynamics (10 HIFLUGCS clusters). This is a dynamics channel independent of X-ray hydrostatics.
- Framing: uses non-parametric Jeans modelling and states it is "not biased towards any specific model". It reports that clusters deviate from the galaxy RAR.
- Data released: not stated.
- Fetched: 2026-11-06

### B7. Mistele, Lelli, McGaugh, Schombert et al. 2025, "Mass models of galaxy clusters from a non-parametric weak-lensing reconstruction"
- Journal: Open Journal of Astrophysics (per comments "published in OJAp"; DOI 10.33232/001c.144242)
- arXiv: 2506.13716 https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.13716
- Quote: "Galaxy clusters systematically deviate from the Baryonic Tully-Fisher Relation (BTFR) and the Radial Acceleration Relation (RAR) defined by galaxies"
- Regime: galaxy clusters (CLASH weak lensing, non-parametric).
- Framing: reports the empirical relation. The abstract states that the offset "depends strongly on the gas mass extrapolation at large radii". This is directly relevant to the Atlas's assumption sliders.
- Data released: not stated.
- Fetched: 2026-11-06

### B8. Chae, Bernardi, Dominguez Sanchez & Sheth 2020, "On the Presence of a Universal Acceleration Scale in Elliptical Galaxies"
- Journal: ApJL 903, L31 (2020)
- arXiv: 2010.10779 https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.10779
- Quote: "all 387 data points from the radial bins of the velocity dispersion profiles are consistent with a universal relation"
- Regime: ellipticals (MaNGA + ATLAS3D slow rotators, spherical Jeans).
- Framing: fits a model (Bayesian inference of the acceleration scale) and reports a single scale consistent with rotating galaxies.
- Data released: not stated.
- Fetched: 2026-11-06

### B9. Scarpa, Marconi, Gilmozzi & Carraro 2007, "Using globular clusters to test gravity in the weak acceleration regime"
- Journal: ESO Messenger 128, 41 (2007)
- arXiv: 0707.2459 https://arxiv.org/abs/0707.2459
- Quote: "their velocity dispersion profile flattens out at large radii where the acceleration of gravity goes below 1e-8 cm/s/s"
- Regime: globular clusters (velocity-dispersion profiles at low acceleration). This is not a g_obs–g_bar plot as such.
- Framing: tests Newtonian gravity in the low-acceleration regime; it suggests "our present understanding of gravity in the weak regime of accelerations is incomplete".
- Data released: not stated.
- Fetched: 2026-11-06

### B10. Huisjes & Hernandez 2026, "Most open clusters follow the radial acceleration relation (RAR) and the baryonic Tully-Fisher relation (BTFR)"
- Journal: none shown (preprint)
- arXiv: 2603.03522 https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03522
- Quote: "We analyse 5646 Gaia DR3 open clusters from the Hunt & Reffert catalogue."
- Regime: open star clusters (parsec scale), placed on the RAR plane.
- Framing: compares the clusters with Newtonian and MOND expectations; the abstract interprets the result "within a MOND framework". Not peer-reviewed per the page.
- Data released: not stated.
- Fetched: 2026-11-06

## C. Open databases

### C1. Lelli, McGaugh & Schombert 2016, "SPARC: Mass Models for 175 Disk Galaxies with Spitzer Photometry and Accurate Rotation Curves"
- Journal: AJ (per comments "Accepted for publication in AJ"; DOI 10.3847/0004-6256/152/6/157)
- arXiv: 1606.09251 https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.09251
- Quote: "a sample of 175 nearby galaxies with new surface photometry at 3.6 um and high-quality rotation curves from previous HI/Halpha studies"
- Framing: database and mass models. It states the stellar mass-to-light assumption explicitly (0.5 Msun/Lsun at 3.6 µm) and shows its effect.
- Data released: yes. The abstract says "The SPARC data are publicly available" and the comments say "All data are available at" http://astroweb.cwru.edu/SPARC/ (link taken from the arXiv page; the site itself could not be fetched from here).
- Fetched: 2026-11-06

### C2. Baumgardt & Hilker 2018, "A catalogue of masses, structural parameters and velocity dispersion profiles of 112 Milky Way globular clusters"
- Journal: MNRAS 478, 1520 (per comments); DOI 10.1093/mnras/sty1057
- arXiv: 1804.08359 https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.08359
- Quote: "We have determined masses, stellar mass functions and structural parameters of 112 Milky Way globular clusters"
- Framing: catalogue; fits a model (N-body grid fitted to velocity-dispersion and surface-density profiles).
- Data released: not stated in this abstract. The companion paper Hilker, Baumgardt, Sollima & Bellini 2019 (arXiv 1908.02778, IAU Symp. 351, DOI 10.1017/S1743921319006823, also fetched 2026-11-06) states for 156 clusters: "The new fundamental parameters of GGCs are publicly available via an online database, which will regularly be updated" (https://people.smp.uq.edu.au/HolgerBaumgardt/globular/; not fetched).
- Fetched: 2026-11-06

### C3. Pace 2025, "The Local Volume Database: a library of the observed properties of nearby dwarf galaxies and star clusters"
- Journal: Open Journal of Astrophysics (per comments; DOI 10.33232/001c.144859)
- arXiv: 2411.07424 https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.07424
- Quote: "a catalog of the observed properties of dwarf galaxies and star clusters in the Local Group and Local Volume"
- Framing: database (positional, structural, kinematic, chemical and dynamical parameters), versioned and community-contributed.
- Data released: yes. "The LVDB catalogs and package are publicly available as a GitHub repository" (https://github.com/apace7/local_volume_database; returned 403 from here, not fetched).
- Fetched: 2026-11-06

### C4. Ettori, Ghirardini, Eckert, Pointecouteau et al. 2019, "Hydrostatic mass profiles in X-COP galaxy clusters"
- Journal: A&A 621, A39 (2019)
- arXiv: 1805.00035 https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.00035
- Quote: "We present the reconstruction of hydrostatic mass profiles in 13 X-ray luminous galaxy clusters"
- Framing: data product plus model comparison. It fits NFW and other mass models, and compares with Emergent Gravity and MOND.
- Data released: yes. The comments say "X-COP data products are available for download on" https://www.astro.unige.ch/xcop (not fetched).

## Unverified candidates and gaps

- Landing pages of the databases: http://astroweb.cwru.edu/SPARC/, https://people.smp.uq.edu.au/HolgerBaumgardt/globular/, https://github.com/apace7/local_volume_database (HTTP 403) and https://www.astro.unige.ch/xcop. All were unreachable from this environment: curl returned 000 or 403, and WebFetch reported them as not allowlisted. Their licences, formats and current versions are therefore unverified. These must be checked before the Atlas links or cites them as data sources.
- KiDS-1000 lensing-RAR data products (Brouwer et al. 2021): whether they were released is not stated on the arXiv page, and no data page was checked.
- Dwarf spheroidals on the RAR as a dedicated compilation: arXiv searches found none beyond A1 (Lelli+2017, 62 dSphs) and McGaugh & Wolf 2010 (BTFR, not RAR). There may be one that the searches missed.
- Globular clusters placed directly on the g_obs–g_bar plane: none found. The verified globular-cluster items (B9, Hernandez & Jimenez 2012) report flattening of the velocity dispersion, not RAR points.
- Neutral review: every review found (D1–D3, Famaey & Durakovic 2025) is built around a named account. No account-neutral review of the acceleration discrepancy across scales was found.

### Observation for the Atlas page
None of the verified items puts globular clusters, dwarfs, disc galaxies, galaxy-scale lensing and galaxy clusters on one g_obs–g_bar plot with both channels kept separate. A1 covers galaxies only. A2 covers BCGs and clusters. A3 is a mass-ratio plot, not an acceleration plot. D2 covers galaxies plus the Solar System. This is the gap the Atlas fills. State it on the page only as a description of the verified list, and only after the unverified checks above are done.
