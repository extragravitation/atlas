# Method gaps (reader B, aeg-d-gc-baumgardt/v3, rows m000066 NGC 6712 and m000020 NGC 5139)

No step was blocked, so no row is marked "method incomplete". These are the steps I had to infer or decide myself. The ones marked (no effect) do not change the result at the precision reported.

1. **Row role not stated.** rows.json does not say whether each row is the default or an alternative row. Its only hint is the source_ref "(RV)". I assumed both are default RV rows. For NGC 5139 the other line-of-sight candidate, K18, has coverage 0.198, so it cannot be plotted and RV would be chosen anyway.
2. **Start of the King integration.** §4.1 gives W(0)=W₀ and dW/dx(0)=0, plus a start at x=10⁻⁶. It does not say whether W=W₀ and W′=0 are applied at x=10⁻⁶ or a series expansion is used. I applied them at x=10⁻⁶. (No effect: the tidal radius changes by less than 10⁻⁶.)
3. **Normalisation of ρ.** The Poisson equation uses ρ(W)/ρ(W₀). I took ρ(W) as given, without its constant prefactor, and clipped it to 0 for W<0. (No effect: light fractions are ratios.)
4. **Projection numerics.** The method says "trapezoidal rule for projections" but gives no grid, no interpolation between RK4 nodes, and no treatment of the integrable square-root behaviour at r=R. I used the trapezoidal rule on the RK4 grid for L(<r). For the cylinder term I used adaptive quadrature on a cubic spline of ρ(ln x), with the substitution x=R cosh u. The method allows any scheme accurate to about 10⁻⁴. I checked convergence: going from 400 to 1600 steps per decade changes F by about 10⁻⁶. As a check, L(<r_t) from the trapezoidal sum agrees with −x²W′ from Poisson to better than 10⁻⁵.
5. **Is the F argument r/r₀?** §4.2 says R is in arcsec, "the unit of r₀", and §4.1 defines x=r/r₀. I inferred that F is evaluated at edge/r₀, with r₀ in arcsec from the MvdM row. The bin radii in veldis.html are also in arcsec, so the MvdM distance (Dist) does not enter the weights. As a check, the computed log(r_t/r₀) matches the MvdM `Conc` column: 1.050 against 1.05 for NGC 6712, and 1.306 against 1.31 for NGC 5139.
6. **Which σ column.** §2 names "σ". I used the column headed σ and ignored Δσ Up and Δσ Low, which only enter §8. The locator for the σ inputs lists "the dispersion, Δσ Up and Δσ Low" together.
7. **Parsing the HTML table.** The method says the name "carries down" to the rows below. It does not say where one cluster's rows end. I took them to end at the next row whose first cell is non-empty. The HTML has stray `<tr><tr><td>&nbsp;</td></tr>` spacer rows, which I skipped because they have fewer than 7 cells.
8. **Harris name matching.** I matched by the ID column ("NGC 5139", "NGC 6712"), normalised with norm_name. These are straightforward; I did not need the aliases. I could not check v1's read_harris behaviour; I followed only the description.
9. **Inputs listed by the locators but not used by the method:** Harris V_t and MvdM `(M/L)2`. I recorded them and did not use them. The models table also has its own `M/L` column, which equals `(M/L)1` for NGC 5139 (1.872). The method says to use the clusters table, so I did.
10. **Bins beyond r_t.** §4.4 says "a bin beyond r_t contributes only the light inside r_t". I implemented this as F(R)=1 for R ≥ r_t. No edge in either row reaches r_t: NGC 5139's last edge is 2034″ against r_t = 2855″, and NGC 6712's is 301″ against r_t = 579″. (No effect.)
11. **Precision of the reported values.** The method gives no rounding rule. I report 5 significant figures.
